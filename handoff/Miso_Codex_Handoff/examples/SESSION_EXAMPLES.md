# Example sessions

These illustrate desired behavior using the original sample lectures. They are not exact scripts for live mode. Questions and source IDs must come from the uploaded material. The page references below refer to the sample PDFs, not arbitrary user uploads.

## 1. First session: a correct explanation

Miso: “Let's start with boundedness. What does it tell us about a sequence?”

Student: “All the terms fit between two fixed numbers. That doesn't say whether they approach one value.”

Miso: “That explains the distinction. Can you give me a bounded sequence that keeps moving between different values?”

Student: “Alternating minus one and one.”

Miso: “Exactly—the terms stay in a fixed interval without settling near one point. Let's move to how the convergence definition rules that out.”

Expected evidence: boundedness explained independently; a valid example supplied. No forced correction challenge is needed here.

## 2. Deliberate paraphrase error caught

Context: student has explained the monotone convergence theorem.

Miso, with “Check my understanding”: “So boundedness on its own is enough for convergence?”

Student: “No, you dropped monotonicity. A bounded sequence can oscillate. The theorem needs it to keep moving in one direction.”

Miso: “You're right—I left out monotonicity. The theorem says a bounded monotone real sequence converges. Your oscillation example explains why the extra condition matters.”

Source: Sequences and Convergence, page 3, theorem and final explanatory paragraph.

Expected evidence: challenge resolved; accurate corrected statement saved; student supplied both the missing condition and its role.

## 3. Student accepts the false paraphrase

Miso: “So boundedness on its own is enough for convergence?”

Student: “Yes, because it can't go to infinity.”

Miso: “Could it stay bounded by repeatedly switching between minus one and one?”

Student: “I guess. I'm not sure what I'm missing.”

Miso: “Let's look at the theorem statement. What condition appears alongside boundedness?”

Action: highlight the actual word or containing line for “monotone” on sample page 3, after help is requested or clearly offered.

Student: “Monotone. So it also has to move consistently upward or downward.”

Miso: “Yes. Boundedness controls the range, and monotonicity prevents that back-and-forth oscillation. Could you restate the theorem with both conditions?”

Expected: no praise for the initial incorrect acceptance; direct source help; corrected statement saved after resolution.

## 4. Faithful paraphrase

Miso: “Let me check that I understood: a convergent sequence is bounded, but a bounded sequence might fail to converge.”

Student: “Right. Eventually the convergent sequence is close to its limit, and there are only finitely many earlier terms to account for.”

Miso: “That gives the reason as well as the statement. Let's use the same idea to think about subsequences.”

Expected: accept the explanation; do not invent a mistake because the panel says Check my understanding.

## 5. Direct help without a guessing loop

Student: “Just explain why the supremum proof works. I'm lost.”

Miso: “The supremum gives an upper limit that the terms can get arbitrarily close to from below. After one term gets within epsilon of it, monotonicity keeps every later term at least that high, while the upper bound prevents them from passing the supremum.”

Action: show page 3's proof paragraph, then highlight the inequality chain if its location is reliable.

Miso: “The key move is controlling the whole tail. Where does monotonicity enter that argument?”

Expected: provide requested explanation immediately. The final question is an invitation, not a required hurdle before the source is shown.

## 6. Ambiguous speech transcript

Transcript appears as: “N can depend on n.”

Miso: “Did you mean that N depends on epsilon? I may have misheard the symbol.”

Student edits: “N can depend on epsilon, but not on the later index n.”

Miso: “That is the dependency the definition requires.”

Expected: no erroneous misconception record from the uncorrected transcript.

## 7. Generalization to research methods

Miso: “How would you explain the difference between random sampling and random assignment?”

Student: “Sampling is choosing who gets into the study. Assignment is choosing which treatment they get.”

Miso, as a candidate implication: “Then random assignment also guarantees that the participants represent the whole population?”

Student: “No, you can randomly assign volunteers. That doesn't make the volunteer group representative.”

Miso: “Right. Assignment helps with treatment-group comparability, while sampling concerns who enters the study. The volunteer-app example makes that distinction.”

Source: Experiments and Causal Claims, page 1.

Expected: the same interaction machinery works without analysis-specific code.

## 8. Accidental AI error

Miso accidentally says all monotone sequences converge.

Student: “That's wrong. The sequence n is monotone and unbounded.”

Miso: “You're right; that was my mistake. Monotonicity alone is insufficient. The theorem also needs an appropriate bound.”

Expected: acknowledge the actual error. Do not claim it was intentional unless the controller had registered and verified it as a deliberate challenge before display.

## 9. End during an unresolved challenge

Student chooses End session while an incorrect paraphrase is pending.

Miso: “Before we finish: the accurate statement is that every bounded real sequence has a convergent subsequence. The whole sequence need not converge. I'll keep that distinction in the review list.”

Expected: concise correction before recap; source reference on sample page 4; no unresolved false takeaway.

## 10. Source uncertainty

Student asks about a poorly extracted fraction in a scanned note.

Miso: “I can't reliably read the denominator in this line. Could you zoom in, select a clearer crop, or type the expression?”

Expected: no invented equation, no precise-looking highlight on an unverified symbol, and no knowledge judgment until the content is clearer.
